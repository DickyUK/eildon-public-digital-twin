How this demonstration is calculated

The demonstration uses a generic £2,850 annual reference and diminishing-return savings functions for PV, battery and combined operation. A thermal/wood contribution is modelled separately and a minimum £450 annual cost is imposed. This is an educational sensitivity model, not an investment forecast, engineering design or actual customer billing analysis. Electricity prices, export revenue, charging constraints, seasonal variation and installation costs are not modelled in sufficient detail for purchasing decisions.

How to interpret peak demand and battery sizing

The three peak figures above are populated automatically from a verified, anonymised private calculation covering the latest 35 days. The 30-minute average power is not an instantaneous maximum.

Whole-home grid imports include heating, hot water, appliances and EV charging. They do not measure useful heat output or reveal gross demand supplied behind the meter by solar or a battery. Archived bill-period costs are not interchangeable with calendar-month spend.

About the example home

An anonymised, upgraded UK detached home with an air-source heat pump, underfloor heating, mechanical ventilation with heat recovery and mixed levels of fabric improvement. The building description is intentionally broad: no street address, EPC certificate reference, household identities, room-by-room vulnerabilities, account numbers, meter details or live usage history are included.

This public demonstrator is static and makes no requests to private energy-account services. The private Eildon model remains separate. Figures and animated flows are illustrative, not live readings.
